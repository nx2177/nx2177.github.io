Four Days in Boston on a Budget | First Whale Watch

2025

I took a day off for my birthday and spent four days in Boston.

Day one

Six hours on the bus from New York. I arrived starving in the evening. Chinatown was right there, so I ate Chinese food and walked around.

Day two

In the morning, the Public Garden — like a park at home, shade, boats on the lake, pretty. Under the trees some brown lumps on the ground. I said they were ducks. A friend said roots. Close up, they were ducks, asleep.

Then Boston Common. From the little hill a spread of grass, the skyline at the end. We finally found the start of the Freedom Trail. A man in period dress walked up and shouted “Tour start,” and I jumped.

The Freedom Trail is a red brick line past old buildings. Some need tickets, so we stayed outside; that was fine. Jugglers at Quincy Market. At noon we walked to the North End for Italian food.

Afternoon: Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum and the Museum of Fine Arts. Average, I thought.

At dusk, Revere Beach — a bit dirty, seaweed and shells. Then the backward clock. I stared and still could not see why it was backward. It ran clockwise. Strange.

Day three

Harvard in the morning. A line of people waiting to rub John Harvard’s foot was a bit much, but red brick, grass, blue sky — handsome. The art museum was all right.

Then MIT. The buildings have a weird postmodern beauty. The Great Dome feels like Columbia’s. Across it, the Charles; we walked the river.

Boston Public Library: I was surprised how many Chinese books they hold. If you are not in a hurry you could sit all day. The old building’s gold hall is lovely. Trinity Church and Old South Church are next door.

Day four

Whale watching in the morning. More than an hour out. At first it was dull. Then I wandered to the bow, and standing there was wonderful — the whole body going up and down with the swell. We saw six humpbacks, close along the hull, tails slapping, the boat heaving as if it might go over.

Afternoon, the tea room by the Boston Tea Party museum. No ticket. From the second floor you watch people throw tea chests off a ship. Funny. Then the harbor, which photographs better than I expected. A bonus.

There is not that much to “do.” After a long time in New York, it is another city’s life, that is all.

波士顿四天穷游｜第一次观鲸

2025

生日请了一天假，去波士顿旅游了四天

第一天

从纽约坐了六个小时的公交，傍晚到波士顿的时候快饿扁了，幸好波士顿唐人街就在旁边，于是就去吃了个中餐顺便参观了一下

第二天

早上先去了public garden, 很像国内的公园，绿树成荫，湖上有许多游船，风景秀丽。经过树荫处，地上有些棕色的凸起，我说那是鸭子，朋友说是树根，靠近一看果然是一群鸭子在那睡觉。

然后走到Boston Common，爬上小丘，大片的草地尽头是波士顿的天际线。好不容易找到自由之路的起点，迎面走来一个穿古典制服的人，突然大喊一声tour start，把我吓一跳。

自由之路就是沿着路上的红砖线走，看一些古典建筑，有些要票所以没进去，不过外面看也还行。昆西市场门口有表演杂耍的。到了中午，我们走到了小意大利吃了个意大利菜

下午参观了Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum和Museum of Fine Arts，感觉中规中矩吧

然后傍晚去了Revere Beach，海滩有点脏，布满海草和贝壳，然后走到反方向的钟，问题是我看了半天都没看出来它为什么是反方向的钟，因为它是正常顺时针的[失望R]就很奇怪

第三天

早上先去了哈佛，看到一堆人排队摸约翰哈佛雕像的脚有点难绷，不过红砖绿草配着蓝天白云也挺好看的，哈佛艺术博物馆还可以

然后去麻省理工，建筑有种怪诞的后现代的美。标志性的Great Dome感觉神似哥大，对面就是查尔斯河，于是去河边走了走

波士顿公共图书馆，意外发现居然收藏了很多中文书，如果不赶时间可以在那坐一整天，老馆的金色大厅也挺漂亮。旁边就是三一教堂和老南教堂

第四天

早上去观鲸了，船开了一个多小时才到观鲸地点，起初有点无聊，但我无意间走到船头，发现站在船头简直太爽了，整个人随着浪涌忽上忽下，乘风破浪。看到了六条座头鲸，贴着船游过去，还用尾巴拍水，把船颠的像快翻了似的

下午去波士顿倾茶博物馆旁边那个tea room，这个不用买票，可以从二楼俯瞰一堆人在船上扔茶包，挺搞笑的，又去旁边海港走了一下，没想到这海港还挺出片的，算是意外收获

玩是没什么玩的，就是在纽约呆久了，体验一下不同城市生活吧
