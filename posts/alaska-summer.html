Preface: A Summer in Alaska

June–July 2025

I woke in a dim room. The fan turned without changing its note. The heat and damp made it hard to breathe. I thought again of the thin, bone-cold wind on the snow mountains. Outside it was raining. Memory struck the window like rain.

From June 29 to July 8, 2025, I crossed the whole country from New York, eight hours in the air, and chased freedom on Alaska’s roads. Those ten days are enough to remember for a lifetime.

We moved between mountains and rivers into the town of Seward. Eight of us in one Airbnb, sharing a bed with a friend, cooking, washing dishes, playing Avalon. A strange kind of happiness.

We hiked Exit Glacier. The mosquitoes came at us like bombers; we could only keep spraying. The climb itself was not hard. The view was something like Mingyong Glacier in Yunnan.

Then sea fishing. Bald eagles saw us off. The water rose and fell. I caught four rockfish and waited more than half an hour for salmon that never came — so this is what it feels like to go home empty. Back at the Airbnb the floor and the bed still seemed to rock, as if we were still on the boat. Sleep fixed it.

From Talkeetna we took a small plane over Denali, the highest peak in North America. I got the copilot’s seat, a first-person view of flying. The scale of it cannot be put into words.

On Matanuska Glacier I wore crampons for the first time. We saw the ice funnels people call toilets, and black ice like obsidian.

Kayaking among the ice at Valdez was tiring; my arms felt close to breaking. Ramming friends’ boats was fun anyway. In front of a blue berg our four kayaks lined up for a photograph.

We flew to Utqiagvik, the northernmost city in the country, and found a little Syria in North America, ruined as if after the end of the world. A place fit for exile. Past security, the airport was a crude shed packed with people, like a camp. Standing in that camp we played our last round of Avalon.

It was all like a long film. Even if you fall into the part, you have to come back. All of you were too clever; in Avalon you saw through me at a glance. I would rather have been a fool. Then there would not be so much sorrow of standing close and remaining far.

I think again of that dusk, Independence Day fireworks opening between the mountains at Valdez, brilliant and already falling. I looked out the window and went quiet. You asked what was wrong. I said, this feeling might wait to become remembrance — only, even then, it was already a dream.

阿拉斯加的夏天 序

June–July 2025

在幽暗的房间中醒来，风扇单调地转着，闷热潮湿让人透不过气。不禁又想起雪山上清冷刺骨的风。外面下雨了。回忆像雨点敲打窗棂。

2025年6月29日到2025年7月8日，从纽约跨过整个美国飞了八个小时过来，在阿拉斯加的公路上追逐自由，这十天可以用一生来回忆。

在雪山和河流之间穿行，来到了Seward小镇上，我们八个人住在一个Airbnb里，和朋友挤一张床，大家一起做饭，洗碗，打阿瓦隆，真是一种奇特的体验。

徒步Exit Glacier, 蚊子像轰炸机一样往上扑，只能不停地喷防蚊喷雾，爬山倒不是很难爬，风景类似于云南的明永冰川。

之后去海钓，白头鹰目送我们出海远航，一路风浪起伏。钓到了四条石斑鱼，等了半个多小时没等到三文鱼，原来这就是钓鱼佬空军的感觉。有趣的是回到Airbnb以后还感觉地面和床一直在晃来晃去，像是还在船上，睡一觉才好了。

在talkteena坐小飞机飞越北美最高峰Denali，有幸坐了副驾，体验第一人称开飞机的乐趣，景色之壮观简直无法用语言形容。

在Matanusk Glacier徒步，第一次穿冰爪在冰川上行走，看到了俗称toilet的冰漏斗，以及像黑曜石一般的黑冰。

在Valdez的冰间kayak, 挺累的，感觉手快划断了，但跟朋友互相撞船也挺好玩的。在蓝色的冰块前我们四条船排成一长条拍照。

飞到美国最北端的城市Utqiagvik, 没想到是北美小叙利亚，荒凉破败得像是末日之后的世界。这是一个适合流放自己的地方。机场过了安检之后是一个简陋的棚子，人们挤在里面，像是难民营，而我们在难民营里站着打了最后一局阿瓦隆。

这一切好像一场漫长的电影，即使有些入戏，终究是要回到现实。你们都太聪明，玩阿瓦隆的时候一眼就看破我的身份，而我倒真希望自己是个傻子。就不会有那么多咫尺天涯的惆怅。

又想起那个黄昏，独立日的烟火在Valdez的雪山间绽放，绚丽而短暂地坠落。我望着窗外陷入沉思。你问我怎么了。我说，此情可待成追忆，只是当时已惘然。
